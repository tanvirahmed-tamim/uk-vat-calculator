How to Correct a VAT Error

By the VAT Calculator UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

A VAT error is not always a crisis. Small errors can usually be fixed on your next return, while larger or deliberate errors must be reported separately. The limit depends on the net value of the error and your sales.

In short

You can correct an error on your next VAT return if it is £10,000 or less, or between £10,000 and £50,000 and below 1% of your sales. Larger errors, and deliberate errors, must be notified to HMRC separately. The correction window is normally 4 years.

Who this is for: Any VAT-registered business that has found an error on a past return, whether it underpaid or overpaid VAT.

The limits

The rule turns on the net value of the error: the additional tax due to HMRC minus the tax due back to you. You can correct an error on your next return if the net value is £10,000 or less, or if it is between £10,000 and £50,000 and is less than 1% of the total value of your sales in the return period.

If the net error is over £50,000, or over £10,000 and more than 1% of sales, you must tell HMRC separately. The same applies to any deliberate error, whatever its size. Deliberate errors cannot be netted off against other errors.

The correction window is normally 4 years from the end of the accounting period in which the error was made. Errors older than that are usually out of time.

Correcting on the next return

To correct on your next return, add the net amount to box 1 if you owe tax, or to box 4 if HMRC owes you. Keep a note of the date you found the error, how it happened and the amount involved, and include the adjustment in your VAT account.

Do not simply change the original return. Once a return has been submitted, the correction is made in a later period, and the audit trail should show both the original entry and the correction.

Notifying HMRC

For errors above the limits, use form VAT652 or the online service to tell HMRC about the error. HMRC will calculate the tax, interest and any penalty, and may ask for the supporting records. Telling HMRC before they contact you usually reduces any penalty.

The same route applies if you are not sure which period the error belongs to, or if the error spans several returns. Describe the calculation clearly and attach the relevant invoices or schedules.

Deliberate errors

A deliberate error is one that was made knowingly, or because you were reckless about whether it was correct. It must always be reported separately, even if the amount is small. The penalty can be up to 70% of the tax, or up to 100% if the error was concealed.

Deliberate errors also carry a longer assessment window: HMRC can look back up to 20 years. Taking advice early is usually cheaper than waiting for an inspection.

Interest and penalties

If you underpaid VAT, interest normally runs from the original due date until the tax is paid. A penalty may also apply, depending on whether the error was careless or deliberate and how quickly you disclosed it.

If you overpaid, HMRC may pay repayment interest. The correction itself does not create a penalty, but the behaviour behind the error can. The penalties guide sets out the percentages.

Common mistakes

  • Netting a deliberate error against an innocent one.
  • Changing a submitted return instead of correcting it in a later period.
  • Ignoring the 1% of sales test for errors between £10,000 and £50,000.
  • Assuming the 4-year window runs from the date the error was discovered.

Questions

Can I correct a VAT error on my next return?
Yes, if the net error is £10,000 or less, or between £10,000 and £50,000 and below 1% of your sales. Larger errors must be notified separately.
What is the £50,000 VAT error limit?
The £50,000 VAT error limit is the upper limit for correcting an error on your next return. Between £10,000 and £50,000 you can self-correct only if the error is less than 1% of your sales.
How long do I have to correct a VAT error?
Normally 4 years from the end of the accounting period in which the error was made. Deliberate errors can be assessed for up to 20 years.
Do I have to tell HMRC about a small error?
No. If the net error is within the limits you can correct it on your next return and keep your own record of what happened.
Does HMRC charge interest on a corrected error?
Yes, if you underpaid VAT. Interest normally runs from the original due date until the tax is paid, separately from any penalty.
What form do I use to notify HMRC of a VAT error?
Form VAT652, or the online service for notifying HMRC about errors in a VAT return.

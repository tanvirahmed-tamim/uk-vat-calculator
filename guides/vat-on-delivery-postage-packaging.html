VAT on Delivery, Postage, and Packaging Charges in the UK

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

Delivery is rarely a separate tax question. In most sales the charge follows the goods, which is why a book posted to a customer stays at 0% while the courier invoice behind it carries 20%. This guide separates the two.

In short

Delivery charged as part of a supply of goods follows the VAT rate of those goods: 20% for standard-rated items, 5% for reduced-rate items and 0% for zero-rated items. A separate delivery service for someone else's goods, and a separate packing service, are standard-rated. Royal Mail universal postal services and unused stamps are exempt, but courier and premium services carry VAT.

Who this is for: UK retailers, ecommerce sellers and service businesses that recharge postage, courier costs or packing to customers.

Is there VAT on delivery and postage?

Delivery charged as part of a sale of goods takes the VAT rate of the goods themselves. A standard-rated sale at 20% carries 20% VAT on the delivery charge, and a zero-rated sale carries no VAT on delivery.

The rule comes from VAT Notice 700/24. Where delivery is required under the contract, the supply is one of delivered goods, and it does not matter whether the delivery charge is itemised separately on the invoice.

A delivery service that is genuinely separate, such as carrying somebody else's goods, is standard-rated at 20% regardless of what is in the parcel. So is a separate packing service. The distinction is about what you contracted to supply, not what the paperwork happens to call it.

Royal Mail, stamps and couriers

Postal services provided by Royal Mail under the universal service are exempt from VAT when they are subject to price and regulatory control. The exemption does not extend to similar services from Royal Mail or from other carriers.

VAT treatment of common postage and delivery services
Service VAT position What it means for you
First and second class stamps, franked mail Exempt No VAT to charge or reclaim on the postal service itself
Unused stamps bought from a Post Office No VAT No input tax to reclaim on the purchase
Special Delivery Guaranteed Standard-rated, 20% Outside the universal service, so VAT applies
Parcelforce and commercial couriers Standard-rated, 20% Reclaimable input tax if used for business

Stamps need care. Buying unused stamps carries no VAT, but if a delivery charge you make to your own customer includes the cost of those stamps, you may have to charge VAT on the whole amount, including the stamp cost. The treatment of the stamp purchase does not automatically pass through to your customer.

The separate rules on stamps are in VAT Notice 701/8.

Delivery follows the goods

Where delivery is part of the contract, the whole supply takes the liability of the goods. A zero-rated book posted to a customer stays zero-rated, including the postage charged, and a reduced-rate item keeps 5% on the delivery.

VAT rate on delivery by type of goods
Goods supplied VAT on goods VAT on delivery
Standard-rated goods, most items 20% 20%
Reduced-rate goods, such as child car seats 5% 5%
Zero-rated goods, such as books and children's clothes 0% 0%
Exempt goods Exempt Exempt

The follow-the-goods rule only applies when delivery is included in the contract. If the contract is for goods collected by the customer and you agree to arrange delivery separately for a fee, that fee is a separate standard-rated supply.

The food and drink guide and the zero-rated, exempt and outside scope guide cover which goods sit in each band. Mistakes here usually start with the goods, not with the postage.

Packaging charges

Packaging that is part of the goods follows the goods. A box, insert or protective wrap supplied with a zero-rated item does not turn the sale into a standard-rated one.

A separate packing service is different. If you charge a customer for packing their own goods, or for a handling service that is not part of a supply of goods, that service is standard-rated at 20%. Notice 700/24 treats a separate charge for packing as standard-rated within the UK.

In practice, an ecommerce seller charging "postage and packing" as one line on a standard-rated order is charging 20% on the lot. The split only matters when the goods are reduced-rated, zero-rated or exempt. The VAT for ecommerce guide covers the wider fulfilment picture.

Delivering somebody else's goods

Carrying goods that belong to someone else is a supply of delivery services, not a supply of delivered goods. That service is standard-rated at 20%, even if the goods inside the parcel are zero-rated.

The same logic applies to a business that arranges delivery as agent for a customer. If the contract is to deliver somebody else's goods, the liability does not follow those goods. If the contract is to supply the goods and deliver them, it does.

This distinction matters for marketplaces and fulfilment providers, where one business stores and ships stock owned by another. The storage and shipping charges are standard-rated services in their own right.

If the courier invoice is addressed to your customer rather than to you, check whether you are recharging a cost or supplying the delivery yourself. The answer changes what appears on your invoice and who holds the evidence.

What to show on the invoice

Show the delivery charge as part of the supply, at the same rate as the goods. If delivery is a separate standard-rated service, show it on its own line at 20% and describe it accurately.

Invoice wording for a mixed delivery treatment
Line Net Rate VAT
Books, delivered £120.00 0% £0.00
Gift wrap service £15.00 20% £3.00
Total £135.00 £3.00

Notice that the delivery of the books sits inside the zero-rated line, while the wrapping service is separate. The rounding and mixed invoices guide explains how to handle the arithmetic when one invoice carries several rates.

Is free delivery really free of VAT?

Free delivery is a commercial decision, not a VAT exemption. If you absorb the courier cost into the price of the goods, the customer pays VAT on that higher price at the rate of the goods.

The business reclaims VAT on the courier invoice in the usual way, provided the charge relates to taxable supplies. There is no special rule that blocks the claim simply because the customer was not charged for delivery separately.

Offering free delivery above a threshold changes the price of the goods, not the liability. A zero-rated book sold with free delivery stays zero-rated. A standard-rated item sold with free delivery carries 20% on the whole amount. The trap is treating the courier cost as a separate exempt or zero-rated item when delivery is part of the sale.

Deliveries to customers outside the UK

When goods are exported, the supply of goods is normally zero-rated if you keep evidence that they left the UK within the time limit. Delivery charged as part of that supply is zero-rated with the goods, not standard-rated because the courier happens to be a UK business.

The evidence has to show the goods left the country. A courier tracking record, a customs declaration or a bill of lading can support the zero rating, while an invoice on its own does not prove export. Keep the commercial documents with the VAT records for the same period.

A separate freight service supplied to a customer who is not buying the goods is a different supply and needs its own analysis. Do not assume the zero rate follows the parcel in every arrangement. If you act as agent for the customer, make that clear in the paperwork.

Sales to the EU need extra care at the border, and sales to Northern Ireland follow different rules again. The imports and exports guide and the Northern Ireland trade guide set out the detail.

Delivery charges on marketplace sales

Online marketplaces can be treated as the supplier for VAT purposes on certain sales, which means the marketplace accounts for VAT on the full amount the customer pays, including any delivery charge. The seller then invoices the marketplace, or is paid net of the tax, depending on the arrangement.

For pricing, a delivery charge shown at checkout is part of the taxable value. It cannot be used to shift value out of the sale or to reduce the VAT due. Sellers who set delivery prices to recover costs should check the marketplace's own VAT statement rather than assume their domestic treatment carries over.

The VAT for ecommerce guide covers deemed supplies and fulfilment.

Showing delivery charges to customers

Consumers must see the total price they pay, including VAT and any compulsory delivery charge. A price that only becomes clear at checkout, after a delivery fee is added, risks breaching the pricing rules even when the VAT itself is correct.

Business customers can be shown delivery net of VAT, provided the rate and amount are stated. Keep the treatment consistent with the goods: if delivery follows a zero-rated item, the quote should say the delivery is zero-rated too.

Where delivery is optional, show it as an option with its price rather than folding it into a headline figure the customer cannot avoid. Where it is compulsory, include it in the headline price. That single choice does more for trust than a long footnote.

Keep the VAT wording next to the delivery figure, not buried in terms and conditions. A customer who has to hunt for the tax treatment will assume the worst.

Returns, redeliveries and recharged costs

A returned parcel is not automatically a VAT event. The original sale stands until it is cancelled or credited, and any delivery charge you refund follows the treatment of the original supply.

If you recharge the cost of collecting a return, decide what you are charging for. A collection service is a supply of delivery, normally standard-rated at 20%. A redelivery of replacement goods under warranty is usually part of the original supply, so it takes the rate of those goods.

Damaged goods replaced free of charge do not create a new sale. The replacement follows the original supply, and no extra output tax is due simply because a second parcel went out. Record the reason for the replacement so the stock records and the VAT records agree.

Where a customer cancels within their rights and you repay the delivery charge, adjust the output tax in the period of the credit note, not in the original return.

Reclaiming VAT on postage and couriers

VAT on standard-rated courier, parcel and delivery charges is reclaimable as input tax when the cost relates to your business supplies. Exempt postal charges carry no VAT, so there is nothing to reclaim.

Keep the courier invoice in the business name where possible. A receipt showing only a postage counter sale at a Post Office will not support an input tax claim, because the underlying postal service is exempt.

If you recharge delivery to customers, the output VAT you charge follows the supply as described above. The input VAT on the courier is a separate claim, and it is normal for the two amounts to differ.

Common mistakes

These are the errors that most often surface in a review of delivery charges.

  • Charging 20% on delivery of zero-rated books or children's clothes when delivery is part of the contract.
  • Assuming a Royal Mail exemption covers Special Delivery or Parcelforce.
  • Reclaiming VAT on stamps, which carry none.
  • Forcing a zero rate onto a separate courier service because the goods inside are zero-rated.
  • Treating a separate packing or handling fee as part of the goods.

Questions

Is there VAT on postage and Royal Mail stamps in the UK?
Royal Mail postal services under the universal service are exempt from VAT, and buying unused stamps does not attract VAT. Courier services and premium Royal Mail services are standard-rated.
Do UK businesses have to charge VAT on delivery, shipping and packaging charges?
Delivery charged as part of a supply of goods follows the VAT rate of those goods. A separate delivery service and a separate packing service are standard-rated at 20%.
What VAT rate applies when delivering zero-rated or reduced-rate goods?
The delivery follows the goods, so zero-rated goods carry 0% VAT on delivery and reduced-rate goods carry 5%. Standard-rated goods carry 20%.
What is the difference between Royal Mail exempt postal services and commercial courier VAT?
Universal postal services are exempt, while commercial courier services are standard-rated at 20%. The exemption does not extend to similar services from Royal Mail or other carriers.
How should VAT on postage and delivery appear on a customer invoice?
Show the delivery charge as part of the supply, with the same VAT rate as the goods. If delivery is a separate standard-rated service, show it at 20% on its own line.
Can VAT-registered businesses reclaim VAT on postage and courier costs?
VAT on standard-rated courier and delivery charges is reclaimable as input tax when it relates to business supplies. Exempt postal charges carry no VAT to reclaim.
Are delivery charges for exempt goods exempt from VAT?
Yes. When delivery is part of an exempt supply, no VAT is charged on the delivery. The same follow-the-goods rule applies to standard, reduced and zero rates.

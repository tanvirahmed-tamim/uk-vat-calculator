VAT Across the UK: England, Scotland, Wales and NI

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

One VAT system covers all four nations. The differences you encounter are usually devolved taxes, income tax powers or post-Brexit goods rules, not VAT itself.

In short

VAT rates and thresholds are the same in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have devolved property and income taxes, and Northern Ireland has special goods rules under the Windsor Framework.

What is the same

VAT is a reserved tax set by the UK government and administered by HMRC. The standard rate (20%), reduced rate (5%), zero rate (0%), registration threshold (£90,000), deregistration threshold (£88,000) and scheme rules apply across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

A business registered in one nation can trade in the others under the same VAT number. There is no additional VAT registration for crossing a UK internal border.

England

England uses the UK-wide VAT rules without a devolved VAT variation. Property transactions are subject to Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT), which is not VAT. Business rates are a separate local tax, collected by councils, with rateable values set by the Valuation Office Agency.

If you sell goods or services in England from elsewhere in the UK, the place of supply rules decide whether UK VAT applies. For most domestic business-to-consumer sales, it does.

Scotland

VAT is the same. Scotland has devolved powers over Income Tax rates and bands, and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) replaces SDLT on property purchases and leases. Scottish Income Tax is not VAT, even though both appear on business costs.

Business rates are set by the Scottish Government and collected by local councils, with properties valued by the Scottish Assessors. They are not VAT. The Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR) regulates charities in Scotland, but VAT reliefs for charities are UK-wide and administered by HMRC.

Tourism, hospitality, financial services, legal services and construction are all significant in Scotland. Those sectors do not change the VAT rate; they change which rules and notices a business meets most often.

Wales

VAT is the same. Wales has the Welsh rates of Income Tax, set by the Welsh Parliament, and Land Transaction Tax (LTT) replaces SDLT. Neither is VAT.

Non-domestic rates in Wales are set by the Welsh Government and collected by local councils. They are a property tax, not a VATable supply, so there is no VAT to reclaim on the rates bill.

The Welsh language does not change VAT treatment. If you issue invoices in Welsh and English, the VAT content must still meet the same invoice requirements.

Northern Ireland

VAT is the same, but Northern Ireland has special arrangements for goods under the Windsor Framework. Some movements of goods between Great Britain and Northern Ireland are treated differently from wholly domestic supplies.

Businesses may hold an XI VAT number for certain movements of goods. The XI prefix identifies a Northern Ireland registration for VAT purposes involving goods. It does not mean a different VAT rate.

SDLT applies to property purchases in Northern Ireland, and business rates are handled by Land & Property Services (LPS). Neither is VAT, although VAT can apply to professional fees and to opted commercial property.

The Framework also affects customs and some product rules. Those are separate from the VAT calculation itself, which still uses the standard, reduced or zero rate as appropriate.

A property purchase can therefore involve VAT on fees, SDLT or LBTT or LTT on the purchase, and business rates on the property. Only one of those is VAT.

Cross-border trade

Within the UK, moving goods between England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland does not create an import or export for VAT in the ordinary sense. The special Northern Ireland goods rules are the main exception to be aware of.

For sales to or from other countries, the place of supply rules decide whether UK VAT applies. Exports are generally zero-rated, imports may attract import VAT, and cross-border services follow their own rules. Postponed VAT accounting can let registered importers declare and recover import VAT on the same return.

Local detail that changes the answer

VAT does not change at a UK border, but three local things do change what a business actually pays and which rules it meets: the property transaction tax, the body that sets and collects business rates, and the mix of sectors that dominate the local economy.

  • England — SDLT on property purchases; business rates collected by councils, with rateable values from the Valuation Office Agency.
  • Scotland — LBTT on purchases and leases; business rates collected by councils, with values from the Scottish Assessors; OSCR regulates charities while VAT reliefs still come from HMRC.
  • Wales — LTT on property purchases; non-domestic rates set by the Welsh Government and collected by councils.
  • Northern Ireland — SDLT on property purchases; business rates handled by Land & Property Services.

Sector mix matters because it decides which VAT rules a business uses most: financial and professional services in London and Edinburgh, hospitality and tourism across the nations, agri-food and cross-border trade in Northern Ireland, and construction everywhere. None of that changes the rate; it changes which notice applies.

Questions

Are VAT rates the same in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?
Yes. VAT is a UK-wide tax set by the UK Parliament, so the rates and registration threshold are the same in all four nations. Devolved taxes such as Scottish income tax and Welsh land transaction tax are separate.
Does Northern Ireland follow different VAT rules?
For most supplies, Northern Ireland follows the same VAT rules as the rest of the UK. Goods movements under the Windsor Framework have specific rules, particularly for goods at risk of entering the EU.
Do Scottish VAT rules differ for land and property?
VAT on land and property follows UK-wide rules. Scotland's Land and Buildings Transaction Tax replaces stamp duty but has no effect on VAT treatment.
